Ardu's Lab — interactive coding simulations

Interactive lessons I built to teach coding through everyday stories: variables as labelled lunchboxes, if decisions, and for loops, plus a night light, a parking sensor, and a predict-the-robot game. The Arduino code highlights each line as it runs, and students guess what happens next.

Differentiated assessment

MYP 3 · Criterion A

For the MYP 3 unit Sustainable cities and communities, I created three levels of the Criterion A (Inquiring and analysing) template, each with a matching task-specific clarification. Every student works towards the same criterion, with scaffolding that matches where they are.

Level 1 · bands 1–4

The most scaffolded version, with sentence starters, guiding descriptors, and hints for each strand.

ScaffoldedSentence starters
Template ↗ Task-specific clarification ↗

Level 2 · bands 5–6

Students explain the need for a solution for a specific client or target audience and plan their research, with some guidance.

GuidedClient focus
Template ↗ Task-specific clarification ↗

Level 3 · bands 7–8

Minimal scaffolding. Students explain and justify the need, present proof of the problem, and build a detailed, prioritised research plan.
