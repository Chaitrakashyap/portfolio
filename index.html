My philosophy of design

Design is a mindset, not just a skill.

Every student learns the power they hold, so inclusion becomes the norm, not an afterthought.

  1. (01)

    Design that brings dignity

    When a product shuts someone out, it quietly tells them they don't belong. A colour-blind person who can't use an app feels incapable, and that chips away at their self-esteem. Whether it's a community space or an app, good design makes everyone feel valued and makes room for everyone.

  2. (02)

    Inclusion as the default

    Inclusive design shouldn't be a special consideration added at the end. From the very first project, students learn to think and design for everyone, asking "can everyone use this?" from the first sketch.

  3. (03)

    Everything is connected

    Good designers stay aware of society, politics, and the environment, and of how they are interconnected. Every design decision touches people and the planet.

  4. (04)

    Seeing how the world works

    Design is shaped by forces far beyond the drawing board: economics, culture, politics, and the environment. When students study examples like planned obsolescence, they see how products are made to be replaced, and who pays the price. Understanding how the world runs, and the part designers play in it, shapes how they think when they design.

Teaching philosophy

The beliefs that shape how I plan, teach, and assess.

  1. (01)

    Inquiry before answers

    Students learn best when they start with a real question. I design hands-on, inquiry-based units where students investigate, test, and discover, so they learn to think critically rather than simply receive information.

    Inquiry-based learningCritical thinking
  2. (02)

    Model, test, build, compare

    I bring real engineering tools into Design. Students model their ideas in CAD, test them with FEA simulation, then build physical prototypes and compare the results, exploring concepts by adjusting and observing, not just reading about them.

    CADFEA simulationPrototyping
  3. (03)

    Design for real people

    "Don't design for a user you imagined. Design for a user you understood." User-centred and community-centred problem solving keeps every project grounded in a genuine need and connects classroom learning to the real world.

    User-centred designReal-world context
  4. (04)

    Every learner, meaningful feedback

    I differentiate tasks, scaffolds, and support for students of varying abilities, and give regular, meaningful feedback against IB assessment criteria so every student knows their next step.

    DifferentiationIB assessment
  5. (05)

    Student agency

    As a Personal Project Supervisor, I guide students through independent design investigations, from identifying a problem to prototyping and presenting, so they own their process and their decisions.

    Personal ProjectIndependence
  6. (06)

    Design that asks big questions

    I integrate Theory of Knowledge into DP Design: students take positions on design claims, analyse real cases, argue as different knowers, and build their own knowledge questions.

    TOKArgumentEthics
