From clue to prototype

Pick a mystery user. Follow the evidence through all five stages of the design process and finish with a virtual prototype you can present and defend. Don't design for a user you imagined. Design for a user you understood.

Teacher overview: lesson map

Groups of 3–4, one mystery user per group. Eight cards means up to eight groups; repeat cards for larger classes and let same-user groups compare personas in E2. Every activity below is tagged with its syllabus statement and command term. Student work saves in each device's browser, so ask one scribe per team to keep the journal on a single device, then print or screenshot it as evidence.

A1

Persona detective

2.1.5Identify25 min
  1. Read your case file. Use only the evidence provided. No guesses yet.
  2. Sort every clue: tap a clue, then tap the box it belongs in. Tap a placed clue to send it back.
  3. Write three assumptions about what your user needs, then prove or disprove each with evidence.
  4. Build your persona. The quote must come from the evidence, not from your imagination.

A2

Research sorter

2.1.2Distinguish10 min

For each piece of research, decide who collected it (primary or secondary) and what kind of data it is (qualitative or quantitative). Then check.

A3

Collect first-hand data

2.1.3ApplyAnalyse35 min
  1. Plan five interview questions. Mix open questions (stories, feelings) with closed ones (numbers, ratings).
  2. Role-play: one teammate becomes your user using only the case file; swap with another team for a cold interview if possible. Better still, interview a real person who fits the user group.
  3. Run one quick measurement or observation. Examples: time how long it takes to find an item, weigh a bag, count the steps in a task.
  4. Analyse: what did you learn that the case file didn't tell you?
QuestionTypeAnswer we got
Teacher note: 2.1.3 lists observations, interviews, surveys, questionnaires, focus groups, material testing and product analysis. Make each team name which methods they used. A good extension is a 5-question class survey on a shared form, which gives quantitative data quickly.

B

Define

Turn your research findings into a clear design challenge and requirements you can test against.

B1

Problem statement builder

2.1.8Explain20 min

A good problem statement names the user, the need and the evidence. It does not name the solution yet.

Teacher note: The checker flags product nouns ("bag", "chair", "opener"). Discuss why "a backpack that…" closes off ideas that "a way to carry… without…" keeps open.
B2

Design specification

2.1.9Construct40 min

Write success criteria for the redesign. Every line needs a source in your research and a way to test it. The checker flags vague words that have no measurable value.

#CategorySpecificationEssential or desirableEvidence it comes fromHow we'll test it

Teacher note: Aim for 8–10 lines with at least 5 essential. Weak: "The product should be comfortable." Strong: "Contact pressure on the shoulder must not exceed the current bag's when carrying a 6 kg load (pressure-sensor or user rating test)." Specification numbers are reused in C2, D2 and E1.
C

Ideate

Explore distinct ways to solve the problem, then evaluate and refine them against your specification before you commit.

C1

Crazy 8s and SCAMPER

2.1.10Apply30 min
  1. Fold an A3 sheet into eight. Each person sketches eight ideas in eight minutes, one per minute. Quantity over quality.
  2. Stuck? Spin a SCAMPER prompt.
  3. As a team, write the eight most different ideas below. Different means a different principle, not the same idea in another colour.

C2

Evaluation matrix

2.1.11Compare20 min

Score each shortlisted idea against your essential specifications (0 = doesn't meet, 3 = fully meets). The matrix pulls from B2 and C1 automatically.

Teacher note: The highest score isn't automatically the winner. Ask teams to justify the choice against the user's main frustration, then combine strengths from the runners-up. That combination is the "iterative analysis" in 2.1.11.
D

Develop

Improve the solution through testing, refinement and technical communication.

D1

Mock-up and user test

2.1.13Create60 min
  1. Choose the lowest fidelity that can answer your question. Card, foam, tape and string are enough for most first tests.
  2. Build the mock-up at full scale where size matters.
  3. Test it with an end-user (or a classmate acting as your persona, following the case file) and measure something. A model without performance data is a sculpture.
D2

Model, test, refine log

2.1.12Demonstrate60 min

Record every loop. Keep going until all essential specifications are met or you can explain why one can't be.
